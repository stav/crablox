# 49.0

## Manufacturing ISM Update: June 2025

Released 1st July 2025

### 🔑 Headline: 49.0 (up from 48.5 in May)

📈 Direction: Contracting

The PMI print is slightly higher this month, but still remains in contraction for the 4th month in a row. Besides that, not much has changed with the component trends. Contracting Slower, New Orders & Employment contracting faster, Prices increasing faster.

### The Key Numbers
- **Manufacturing PMI**              : 49.0 vs 48.5 (+0.5) <br> → Contracting Slower
- **New Orders**                     : 46.4 vs 47.6 (-1.2) <br> → Contracting Faster
- **Production**                     : 50.3 vs 45.4 (+4.9) <br> → Growing From Contracting
- **Employment**                     : 45.0 vs 46.8 (-1.8) <br> → Contracting Faster
- **Supplier Deliveries**            : 54.2 vs 56.1 (-1.9) <br> → Slowing Slower
- **Inventories**                    : 49.2 vs 46.7 (+2.5) <br> → Contracting Slower
- **Customers’ Inventories**         : 46.7 vs 44.5 (+2.2) <br> → Too Low Slower
- **Prices**                         : 69.7 vs 69.4 (+0.3) <br> → Increasing Faster
- **Backlog of Orders**              : 44.3 vs 47.1 (-2.8) <br> → Contracting Faster
- **New Export Orders**              : 46.3 vs 40.1 (+6.2) <br> → Contracting Slower
- **Imports**                        : 47.4 vs 39.9 (+7.5) <br> → Contracting Slower

#### Summary:
- New Orders and Backlogs Contracting
- Production Growing and Employment Contracting
- Supplier Deliveries Slowing
- Raw Materials Inventories Contracting; Customers’ Inventories Too Low
- Prices Increasing; Exports and Imports Contracting

#### Key Industry Comments:
The commentary remains dominated by tariff uncertainty and supply chain disruptions. Manufacturers are reporting that "tariffs continue to impact material pricing," whilst others note that "geopolitical tensions and volatile thinking from China in the US tariff policy make it difficult to plan." Interestingly, Middle East tensions emerged as a new subject in June comments, though there's no indication of related supply chain disruptions yet.

### Trading Implications

The standout story here is production finally breaking above 50 for the first time in months, jumping 4.9 points to 50.3. That's a proper shift and suggests companies are starting to work through some of their operational bottlenecks.

**But here's the rub** - new orders dropped another 1.2 points to 46.4, marking the fifth consecutive month of decline. Employment also deteriorated further to 45.0. So we've got production recovering whilst forward-looking demand indicators continue weakening.

The massive 17.3 point drop in the prices index to 52.1 is the most significant development here. That's inflationary pressure finally starting to ease meaningfully after months of elevated readings - though at 52.1, prices are still increasing, just at a much slower pace.

Export orders improved significantly (+6.2 to 46.3) and imports jumped 7.5 points to 47.4, suggesting some of the international trade disruption is beginning to normalise, though both remain in contraction territory.

#### Bottom Line:
Companies managing through demand uncertainty by optimising production whilst remaining cautious about future commitments. The production recovery is encouraging, but the employment and new orders trends suggest businesses aren't convinced this improvement is sustainable.
