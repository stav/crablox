# 50.8

## ISM Services PMI
### Expanding
for June 2025

🏄‍♂️ NMI Cross-Currents

After that mixed manufacturing print we dissected earlier, the Services PMI has come through with something a bit more encouraging. We're back above 50 at 50.8 versus 49.9 in May, beating the 50.5 consensus and marking a proper bounce after just one month in contraction.

This creates an interesting dynamic when you layer it against both the manufacturing struggles and that NFP surprise that shifted the Fed narrative.

#### What Caught My Eye:
Services PMI hit 50.8 (+0.9) whilst business activity absolutely surged to 54.2 from 50.0 - that's a 4.2 point jump that suggests genuine momentum rather than statistical noise. New orders telling an even better story at 51.3, up a massive 4.9 points from 46.4.

But here's where it gets interesting against our earlier manufacturing discussion - employment in services dropped 3.5 points to 47.2, shifting into contraction territory. So whilst manufacturing employment was already weak, services companies are now joining the cautious hiring theme despite stronger demand.

Inventories rebounded nicely to 52.7 (+3.0) and imports recovered to 51.7 (+3.5), suggesting companies are restocking after May's pullback. Export orders also improved to 51.1 (+2.6), which contrasts with manufacturing's export struggles.

Prices remain sticky at 67.5, down just 1.2 points but still well elevated compared to that massive manufacturing price deceleration we saw earlier. This goods-services inflation split is becoming quite pronounced.

#### Sector Performance:
The breadth here is actually quite impressive - 10 sectors expanding versus 6 contracting. Other Services, Transportation & Warehousing, and Utilities leading the charge, whilst Educational Services, Professional & Technical, and Health Care are pulling back.

The commentary continues to be dominated by tariff uncertainty, with Middle East tensions now entering the conversation (though no supply chain impacts so wouldn't worry here). Firms keep mentioning "slow growth and economic uncertainty" even amid this recovery.

#### Trading Context:
When you step back and consider this alongside the manufacturing data and that jobs surprise, we're seeing a genuine two-speed economy emerge. Services demand is recovering whilst manufacturing stays defensive. Employment across both sectors remains cautious despite demand improvements.

This supports the narrative that businesses are managing through uncertainty rather than positioning for sustained growth. The NFP strength combined with this services resilience keeps the Fed in a tricky spot. They can't ignore labour market tightness, but they also can't dismiss the PMI prices indexes staying elevated.

The services bounce provides some economic cushion, but the employment trends across both sectors suggest companies aren't convinced about sustainability. Meeting demand ,but stay lean on commitments.

Quite the contrast to manufacturing's production recovery amid order declines. Services showing order recovery amid employment declines. Different sectors, same underlying caution about what's coming next.

These sectoral differences are creating some interesting cross-currents for positioning. 📈
